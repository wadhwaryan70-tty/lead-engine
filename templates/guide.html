How to use

From finding a client to sending the first message.

1. Find clients

On Find clients, pick the countries or regions and press Find clients. The app looks for oil & gas operators, EPC contractors and service companies whose roles have been advertised for 30+ days: a sign they need help hiring. Recruitment agencies are left out.

Each job ad checked uses 1 credit. Start with 25 and grow once you see the results.

2. Review the drafts

Open Results › Drafts. Every contact gets an email and a LinkedIn sequence. HR and talent leaders get the permanent-hire pitch (flat fee of one month's salary); project and operations managers get the contract pitch (man-hour / man-month, 6-month minimum).

Edit anything, then Save. Use Open in my email app or Copy email to send.

How drafts are written

The method follows the Art of Selling: the only goal of outreach is a short meeting. Before writing, the app builds a research pack for each contact. Every fact is marked Verified (with its source and date, e.g. the job ad) or Assertion (an industry pattern, used only as a question). Nothing else may be stated as fact.

Each contact gets a full sequence: Email 1 (day 1, opens with their verified hiring signal), Email 2 (day 3, a new angle as a question), Email 3 (day 8, “is this for you, or someone else?”), a LinkedIn note and two follow-ups, and a call script with likely objections.

The score

A separate AI reviewer checks every sentence (Evidence-backed Generic Made up) and scores the sequence out of 100: specific opening, about them not us, no filler, one low ask, each email adds something new. Below {{ THRESHOLD }} it is rewritten with the reviewer's fixes, up to 3 times.

The score helps; you decide. Open Research & score on any draft to see why it scored what it did and what the research is missing.

When they reply

Open They replied, paste their message and press Suggest a response. You get what kind of reply it is, which part they weren't convinced by (Problem, Solution, Proof or Ask), and a short answer: acknowledge, ask a question, reframe, respond. If a meeting looks likely, you also get discovery questions. Nothing is sent.

3. Check the email label

Verified From job ad Company inbox safe to send.

Guessed built from the company's email format but not confirmed. Send these a few at a time; too many bounces can push your emails into spam.

No email use the LinkedIn note and follow-ups instead.

4. Track what you've sent

Change each draft's status to Sent, Replied, Meeting booked or Not interested. Everyone on the team sees the same status, so nobody contacts the same person twice. Anyone who got a draft is skipped for 90 days.

5. Big companies with no contact

Large companies rarely publish emails. In Results › Client companies, use HR leaders, Project leaders or LinkedIn search to find the right person, then Add a person. Under Your lists, press Find emails & write drafts.

6. Old prospects

Upload your old lists under Your lists. Duplicates and “not interested” contacts are removed. Old contacts whose company has 30+ day vacancies right now are marked HOT.

Sending in bulk

Results › Downloads has send lists ready to import into Gmail mail merge, Instantly or Lemlist. Send from a separate outreach email address, and start with 30–50 emails a day per mailbox.

Better drafts

Fill in Settings fully: the sender's name, real proof points (placements, clients you may name) and any replacement guarantee. Drafts only use what's there. After changing Settings, press Re-write drafts with latest settings on a search; drafts already marked Sent or Replied are left alone.

When credits run out

The credit bar on Find clients shows what's left and when it renews. With no credits left, keep working from Your lists: adding people and uploading lists costs nothing.
